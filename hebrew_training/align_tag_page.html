How to mark a boundary

The usual case is accept or nudge one of the two proposals. Move the playhead with the waveform (click, or drag the red playhead), then:

  • Hear the word (w), up to the mark (q) or after it (e), and loop (l / shift+l) to compare.
  • Put the mark at the playhead with f, or drag a blue mark in the lower panel. ←/→ step from mark to mark; tab/shift+tab jump between words.
  • Nudge for fine adjustments: 5ms, 25ms or 125ms steps. When segmentation lines are shown, nudges within 10ms of a line snap to it.

Correcting the transcript

Wrong word? Double-click it to retype, or clear the box to delete it. Use + add word before or + add word after for one the transcript missed — the new word starts selected as "?" so you can type straight over it. Corrected words keep the original and are outlined amber; added ones are outlined green. Both are preserved in the export.

Word boundaries & overlaps

Double-click the start or end label in the info bar (or the buttons) to type an exact time — this may create overlaps. Use untangle words to split overlapping words equally, or reset to baseline to restore the original dataset words and timings.

Using the waveform & spectrogram

Drag a blue mark to move it; drag the middle of a word to slide the whole tag — sliding into a neighbouring word pushes it along (and its own neighbour, cascading) rather than overlapping or shrinking it. Drag any mark to make that word current. Click empty space to move the playhead, or drag the red playhead to scrub. Wheel zooms, shift+wheel scrolls, and the bar under the panel pans left and right. fit word snaps to the current word, fit all words shows the whole sentence.

Segmentation lines

Tick show speech segmentation lines to overlay automatically detected syllable-like boundaries (green). They are only a hint — adjust the sensitivity threshold (higher = fewer lines) and min gap if they do not match the speech.

Saving & done

save keeps a checkpoint of your current work; it is always safe mid-clip. Only done & next clip marks the clip as finished (you can undo with unmark as done) and moves on. The top bar shows how many clips are saved vs done; click the saved count for the full list of your clips and their status. Reloading lands you back on the clip you last saved without marking done.
